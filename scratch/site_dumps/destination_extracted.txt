Sri Lanka, an island in the Indian Ocean, lies to the south of the Indian subcontinent - between 5°55′ and 9°55′ north of the equator, and between eastern longitudes 79°42′ and 81°52′.
The total land area is 65,610 sq. km - astonishingly varied across a length of 445 km and a breadth of 225 km, encompassing tropical beaches, verdant vegetation and ancient monuments. A mountainous mass sits south of centre, with heights exceeding 2,500 metres, surrounded by broad plains.
Palm-fringed beaches surround the island and the sea temperature rarely falls below 27 °C. Coastal temperatures hover between 26 °C and 28 °C, with a mean of 27.5 °C in the capital; in the higher elevations it can be cool, dropping to 16 °C at nearly 2,000 metres. Climatically, Sri Lanka has no off season.
Documented history began with the arrival of the Aryans from North India, who introduced iron, advanced agriculture and irrigation. After Portuguese (1505), Dutch (1656–1796) and British rule, Sri Lanka won back independence in 1948 and is now a sovereign republic within the Commonwealth and the United Nations.
